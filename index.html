THE TV BOOM

How a multibillion-pound television rights deal in 2013 permanently altered the landscape of English football and global player transfers.

Scroll Down to Begin
Chapter 1

The Pre-TV Era

Before the astronomical TV contracts of the 2010s, Premier League clubs spent at a steady, moderate pace. Total league spend hovered below £500M annually, with clubs operating under traditional budget constraints.

Chapter 2

The TV Rights Boom

In 2013, the Premier League signed an unprecedented domestic television rights deal worth billions. This sudden influx of TV cash completely transformed the transfer market, sending annual spending skyward past £1.5B.

Chapter 3

Mapping the Talent Influx

Flush with cash, England became the financial epicenter of the global football economy. English clubs began importing top-tier talent from South America, across continental Europe, and beyond. Hover over flow lines to explore country-to-country transfer volumes, and use the zoom/pan features to inspect details.

Chapter 4

Valuation Explosion

With more money chasing the same elite players, player valuations skyrocketed. The average transfer fee grew by a staggering 190%, making multi-million pound transfers the new standard for even mid-table clubs.

Chapter 5

Investment by Position

Where did this massive wave of cash go? While forwards and midfielders continued to command premium fees, investment in defensive lines also saw dramatic increases, highlighting a league-wide inflation in squad values.

The New Reality of Football

The Premier League's TV boom cemented its position as the wealthiest league in the world. What began as a domestic broadcasting war has permanently reshaped the global football economy, creating a financial divide that remains wider than ever.
